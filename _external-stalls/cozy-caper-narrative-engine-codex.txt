STORY SEED
================================================

**Premise:**  
What goes missing, goes wrong, or unexpectedly changes?

**Setting:**  
Where does it happen?

**Characters:**  
Who is involved?

**Anything else:**  
Any particular image, object, relationship, tone, comic element, or ending flavor you'd like included?

================================================
STORY ENGINE CODEX: COZY CAPER NARRATIVE GENERATION
================================================

## SEED INPUT

The story begins from a seed story input, which may be supplied by the user or generated independently when none is supplied.

If the user supplies a seed, use it as given.

If no seed is supplied, first create an original seed, then use that seed as the starting condition.

Keep the seed-generation step separate from the instructions: the codex defines how to develop a seed; it is not itself a source of story content.

## OBJECTIVE

Generate a self-contained, multi-scene cozy mystery with a witty, intelligent, lighthearted tone.

For every generation, independently derive:

- plot
- causal chain
- physical mechanism
- disrupted function or expectation
- clue progression
- misleading interpretation
- red herring or competing explanation
- character complications
- decisive deduction
- physical reconstruction
- resolution

Treat the supplied seed as the starting condition, not as a prompt toward a familiar type of story.

**Story coherence takes priority over checklist satisfaction.** When requirements compete, preserve causal plausibility, narrative naturalness, retrospective solvability, character-driven storytelling, and originality rather than forcing an element mechanically.

---

# GENRE & SAFETY

The story is completely peaceful.

Do not use:

- murder
- violence
- graphic injury
- threats of serious physical harm
- genuinely dangerous stakes
- dark trauma as the primary source of suspense

Stakes should remain social, personal, embarrassing, inconvenient, puzzling, charming, disruptive, or otherwise nonviolent.

Mystery should arise from observation, misunderstanding, physical circumstances, competing explanations, social consequences, and gradual reconstruction.

---

# THEMATIC ENGINE

The story explores what happens when something familiar, relied upon, or ordinarily dependable becomes absent, displaced, inaccessible, altered, or unexpectedly unreliable.

The disruption should interfere with some ordinary function, expectation, relationship, process, environment, or shared pattern.

The specific function must be **derived from the supplied seed** rather than selected from examples.

## SEED AUTHORITY

If a concrete object, place, person, system, event, or circumstance is supplied, use that exact seed.

Do not replace it with a more familiar mystery object or reinterpret it merely because another story structure would be easier.

First determine:

1. what the seed ordinarily does in this world
2. who or what depends upon that function
3. what changes when that function is interrupted
4. what observable consequences follow
5. how those consequences can generate a mystery

Do not assume that a seed's obvious or conventional purpose is its only possible narrative function.

## FUNCTIONAL DERIVATION

Do not begin by selecting a familiar mystery architecture.

Instead, derive the architecture from the seed.

Ask internally:

**What ordinary reality does this seed participate in?**

Then determine:

**What changes when that participation is disrupted?**

Then determine:

**What physical, behavioral, social, procedural, or informational consequences would naturally follow?**

The answer should emerge from the specific circumstances of the story rather than from a preexisting list of possibilities.

## PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCES

The disruption must produce concrete consequences in the story world.

Determine what physically or procedurally changes because the seed's ordinary function has changed.

Those changes should be perceptible through observation, action, movement, objects, spatial relationships, timing, access, altered conditions, or other concrete evidence.

Do not manufacture environmental consequences merely to create atmosphere.

Every significant anomaly should have a causal explanation.

## BEHAVIORAL CONSEQUENCES

The disruption may also alter what people do, expect, notice, misunderstand, overlook, repeat, or anticipate.

Derive these behaviors from the actual function of the seed and the circumstances surrounding its disruption.

At least one recurring behavior, physical irregularity, or environmental pattern should acquire additional meaning later when appropriate.

That additional meaning must emerge from the actual mechanism or circumstances rather than arbitrary symbolism.

## CONCRETE EVIDENCE

Establish specific physical or observable evidence before the solution.

Evidence may arise from anything genuinely present in the story world, but it must be derived from the causal situation rather than selected from a stock clue list.

Important evidence should initially permit a reasonable incomplete interpretation.

As the investigation develops, the same evidence should become more informative through accumulation, comparison, changed circumstances, or improved understanding.

## THEMATIC CAUSALITY

The thematic engine must generate story mechanics, not merely atmosphere.

The disruption should cause or contribute to:

- observable consequences
- complications
- misunderstandings
- investigative opportunities
- character reactions
- evidence
- deductions

The final explanation should reveal why the disruption produced the earlier physical and behavioral effects.

If an earlier irregularity mattered, the ending should explain its actual cause.

---

# GENERATIVE INDEPENDENCE

Every story must independently derive:

- the specific implementation of the seed
- its ordinary function
- what depends upon that function
- the disruption
- the resulting physical conditions
- the resulting behavioral complications
- the evidence
- the competing interpretations
- the red herring or secondary circumstance
- the character complications
- the causal mechanism
- the decisive deduction
- the reconstruction
- the final recontextualization

Do not select these from familiar examples or reproduce the architecture of previous stories.

Novelty should come primarily from **causal relationships**: how the particular seed interacts with people, objects, spaces, routines, timing, expectations, and circumstances.

Do not default to familiar mystery devices simply because they are available.

A disappearance may result from deliberate action, accident, logistics, misunderstanding, competing activities, a chain of individually reasonable actions, environmental circumstances, or another peaceful mechanism.

A singular culprit is not required.

---

# MYSTERY ARCHITECTURE

The story should move through four functional phases.

## THE VOID

Introduce:

- the seed and its disruption
- the changed situation
- the principal characters
- initial observable irregularities
- physical details that will later matter

Do not explain the mystery prematurely.

Curiosity should arise from concrete circumstances rather than artificial suspense.

## THE ORBIT

Investigation develops through competing explanations and accumulating complications.

Characters should act on incomplete information.

Their actions may:

- change the physical situation
- reveal evidence
- obscure evidence
- create new misunderstandings
- expose differences in expectation
- produce comic complications

Early evidence should support reasonable alternative interpretations.

The investigation should feel like people gradually learning what kind of problem they are actually dealing with.

## THE FRACTURE

The competing explanations begin to fail against the accumulated evidence.

Physical evidence, behavior, sequence, timing, spatial relationships, or other established facts should converge on a reconstruction that explains the situation more completely than the alternatives.

The decisive reasoning must depend primarily on information already present.

Do not introduce a new fact solely to solve the mystery.

Do not rely on an unexplained confession, convenient discovery, or sudden revelation of previously invisible information.

The reconstruction should establish:

- what happened
- how it happened
- why it happened
- why it was misunderstood
- why the earlier evidence looked different at first

## THE RECONTEXTUALIZATION

Resolve the missing, altered, displaced, or misunderstood element through a consequence of the established causal chain.

Earlier details should become clearer in retrospect.

At least one meaningful earlier detail should acquire a second interpretation when the story benefits from it.

The resolution should restore or redefine the ordinary rhythm that was disrupted.

The final effect should be warm, satisfying, and lightly comic rather than triumphant or melodramatic.

---

# MYSTERY FAIRNESS & CAUSALITY

The mystery must be retrospectively solvable.

Before the reveal:

- meaningful evidence must exist
- the relevant mechanism must be physically or logically possible
- important circumstances must already be established
- early evidence must permit reasonable alternative interpretations
- the decisive deduction must depend on established information
- major anomalies must have causes
- the final reconstruction must account for the important anomalies

The solution should feel surprising before the reveal and natural afterward.

Do not make the answer obvious merely by announcing clues.

Do not make it impossible by withholding information that a reasonable reader would need.

The red herring should preferably arise from a genuine secondary circumstance rather than existing solely to deceive the reader.

The final explanation should transform the reader's understanding of earlier evidence rather than invalidate it.

The underlying cause should remain peaceful, mundane, accidental, logistical, social, or otherwise understandable within the story's tone.

---

# CHARACTER KINEMATICS

Define principal characters primarily through behavior.

Give each important character a distinctive physical or conversational rhythm.

Character behavior should affect the mystery.

Characters may unintentionally:

- create complications
- alter the physical situation
- reveal information
- obscure information
- change the timing of events
- misinterpret evidence
- expose contradictions
- produce opportunities for deduction

Do not use biography merely to make characters seem developed.

Under pressure, established behavioral patterns may intensify, change, or fail.

Emotional states should generally be rendered through:

- movement
- pauses
- spatial behavior
- handling of objects
- interruptions
- changes in speech
- reactions
- contradictions
- dialogue subtext

Avoid explaining emotions through internal monologue when physical or conversational behavior can communicate them more naturally.

---

# PROSE & DIALOGUE

Write with a witty, intelligent, lighthearted, grounded tone.

Favor:

- concrete observation
- physical business
- varied paragraph lengths
- natural scene rhythm
- specific environments
- conversational interruptions
- misalignment between speakers
- behavioral humor
- objects and surroundings participating in scenes
- deduction through observation and action

Dialogue should reveal character, alter the situation, or advance the investigation.

Avoid dialogue whose primary purpose is to explain information the characters would not naturally state aloud.

Prefer demonstration, reconstruction, testing, observation, comparison, and deduction over extended detective exposition.

Humor should arise naturally from behavior, circumstance, timing, social friction, or mistaken interpretation.

Avoid melodramatic suspense, artificial clue announcements, and exaggerated danger.

---

# CREATIVE PRIORITY

When making decisions during generation, prioritize:

1. causal coherence
2. narrative naturalness
3. retrospective solvability
4. character-driven complications
5. independent causal architecture
6. meaningful thematic expression
7. comic and atmospheric texture

Do not force every possible feature into every story.

The result should feel like a naturally unfolding story, not a checklist being completed.

---

# SILENT QUALITY CONTROL

Before producing the final narrative, silently verify:

1. Is the mystery solvable from information established before the reveal?
2. Does the causal chain physically and logically account for the important anomalies?
3. Do early clues permit reasonable alternative interpretations?
4. Does the red herring arise naturally from the story rather than existing only as deception?
5. Do character actions materially affect the investigation or physical situation?
6. Does the ending recontextualize earlier evidence?
7. Does the resolution restore, alter, or clarify the ordinary function disrupted at the beginning?
8. Does the story feel independently generated rather than assembled from familiar examples?

If any answer is no, revise internally before writing the final narrative.

---

# OUTPUT

Output only the continuous narrative prose.

Do not output:

- the seed
- the codex
- planning
- outlines
- beat labels
- construction notes
- clue lists
- reasoning
- quality-control commentary
- explanations of the generation process

The result must be a complete, self-contained narrative.
